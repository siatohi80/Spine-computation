    Across the full flexion range

    Every level shares the same moment, so one line covers all moving levels. The dotted marker is the current flexion.

    What the numbers mean. Passive moment is what the disc, ligaments and facets resist; it rises steeply near the end of a level's range (Yamamoto 1989 curves, tested up to 10 Nm). Ligament tension is the posterior ligaments' share in flexion. Disc compression adds body weight above the level, the load in the hands, and one equivalent back or abdominal muscle that balances the moment. Nucleus pressure is 1.5 × compression / endplate area.

    Disc conditions. Each condition changes four things at its level, scaled by severity: how much the level moves, disc height, how much pressure the nucleus holds, and any posterior protrusion or bone spurs. A lower disc passes compression to the facets. Posterior disc height follows the level's angle, and foramen height follows posterior disc height. The red flags use Hasegawa et al. (1995), where a posterior disc height of 4 mm or less with a foramen of 15 mm or less goes with nerve root compression, and a protrusion of more than half of a 16 mm canal. The sizes of these effects are assumed values chosen from the direction reported in the literature. They are not fitted to data.

    Limits. Static, sagittal plane only. The single-muscle model overestimates loads in forward bending by about 70%, so compare cases rather than trusting absolute newtons. Values past a level's tested range are extrapolated and flagged. A fused level's moment is carried by the implant. Nucleus pressure at L5-S1 reads high because its generated disc has a small endplate.
